## 0. TL;DR

**`31` diagnosed the failure mode and this session reproduced it one level up.** `31` closed with: *"the thing to fix is not the reversal — it is the confidence with which the spec recorded a decision the user had never seen."* Spec `017` was written on 9/17 from notes `30` and `31`, built through the night of 9/18, migrated, documented into three doc files, redesigned against an approved preview at 11:14, and reported at 2,010 unit tests passing. **The call started at 12:13 and moved the grain of four of its requirements.** Nobody contradicted themselves: **Wendy tested the built model against a real deal and it failed.**

- **⚑⚑⚑ THE CRM ID BECOMES THE CONTRACT, AND SUBMISSION STATUS GOES WITH IT (D17, D18).** `017` **P-02 / FR-002** put Submission status on the submission (`submission.deal_status_code NOT NULL DEFAULT 'IN_PROCESS'`, `0001_initial.py:160-161`, FK to the new `deal_status_kind` at `:133-142`). **Wendy's test case broke it in one sentence:** *"this is going to get wonky because we can have multiple CRM IDs and one was bound and one was not… One was bound, one was lost."* One modeling package, several CRM IDs, different outcomes. Splitting into separate submissions was ruled out on the spot — her Allstate case is *"one modeling project for 8 CRM IDs… she's not going to want to go through that process 8 times"*, and Cheryl called duplication *"cumbersome."* Cheryl set the boundary: ***"your submission status really should go down to the CRM table. The modeling status is fine to have one modeling status."*** They named it between them — Wendy, *"it's really more of a CRM ID status than a definition of a submission in here"*; Cheryl, ***"Contract, it's a contract status."*** → **O32-5**. (§8)

## 5. ⚑⚑⚑ The zero-loss treaty (D8–D11)

### 5.1 What happened

> **Ben:** *"I come and say, okay, I want to export this group at the TY perspective. **The behaviour is `016` FR-007 working as specified** — *"A ticked treaty the table does not hold fails that treaty's row alone, naming the treaty"* — and Ben's point is that **reaching FR-007 is the defect.** *"That's not quite cutting it because we can find ourselves in this scenario… **reaching this scenario is not acceptable, essentially. ### 5.2 The requirement, and the line Cheryl drew

**D11 — the Workbench does not diagnose a zero.** *"There's a number of reasons that that could happen. And **I think it's not the job of the workbench to display why a treaty didn't take loss or why there's no loss.**"* Her examples are the reason: an auto portfolio under a per-risk attaching high enough that *"auto's not going to ever give you any loss for that. I expect that"*; a Caribbean treaty in the group *"because it applied to a different portfolio, but not to this particular one."* **Her conclusion: *"These could be completely valid errors. They could be 100% correct and we wouldn't expect losses."***

**D8 — what the list owes the analyst instead.** *"**What would be helpful is to know in that list to only display options that have loss in the output**… The list that we choose from when we're exporting would tell us: **these are your choices that are available. You only have 3 treaties that took losses, so you can only export these three.**"* And what she would then do: *"I might stop right at that point and be like, oh, okay, don't export. I got to go back to my analysis and figure out why I only have loss for three treaties."*

**D10 — filtered out, not shown as zero.** Ben offered both and answered himself: *"Do you want it to be automatically filtered if that's the case? ### 5.3 The mechanism — and the constitution problem nobody raised

**Ben's first instinct was to read Risk Modeler's own treaty-losses view, and he tested it live and rejected it.** *"There's an option… treaty losses and see the treaties, some subset of the total treaties, which is promising. **However, it's skipping two that actually produced TY losses, which are included in the actual exported data.**"* Cheryl's caution about concluding anything from that is the right one to record: *"I'm hesitant to just say, oh, there's something that's amiss in Risk Modeler because I feel like there's a lot of different ways that you can structure these things that it might be a perfectly valid way to display the results… **The risk modeler results might be perfectly fine for that group. I don't know.**"*

**Her alternative is the design:** *"**Can you show the pure premium in the table?** Because even if you listed them, but if you had the pure premium listed… **maybe that's simpler than trying to dynamically figure out how to adjust that list all the time.**"* Ben: *"That's a good point… **That's genius**… I'm going to call stats. **⚑ The call he named exists, and calling it where he described it is forbidden.** `irp_gateway.py:1319-1325`:

> `def get_analysis_stats(self, *, analysis_id: int, perspective_code: str, exposure_resource_id: int) -> list[dict]:`
> *"GET /platform/riskdata/v1/analyses/{analysisId}/stats — RM's row list verbatim. The wheel validates perspective_code against its own PERSPECTIVE_CODES (T-02); the gateway never bypasses that check."*

**Constitution Article 11, interface contract** (`.specify/memory/constitution.md:402-406`): *"The web layer **MUST NOT** call IRP polling methods (`get_*`) or **result-retrieval methods (`get_elt`, `get_ep`, etc.)**. These are exclusively the domain of the poller and result workers."* **The v4.1.0 request-path carve-out (`:424-430`) is narrower than it sounds:** it permits *"a **bounded, single-analysis** Platform metadata read (`get_analysis_metadata`)… when it answers a point-of-action validation"* and closes by repeating that *"**enumerations and result-retrieval methods (`get_elt`, `get_ep`) stay worker-side**."* **`get_analysis_stats` sits beside `get_analysis_ep` in the gateway protocol (`:591-596`) and is a result-retrieval method by any reading.** Filtering the cart with it is an amendment, not a carve-out — and it would also be N calls for N selected analyses, not one, which is the *bounded* half of the clause.

**The route that needs no amendment is already there.** The treaty list the cart renders is **not** live: it is `describe_run`'s `resolved.treaties`, captured when the analysis's run details are read and stored on the analysis (`irp_gateway.py:420-425`). **Compute the has-loss flag at the same point and store it beside the treaty.** That is worker-side by construction, costs one extra read per analysis rather than one per form render, and makes the cart's filter a pure read of stored data — the same shape `016` P-05 already uses to decide whether TY is offered at all.

**Three things to settle before building it:**

1. **Whether the wheel's `PERSPECTIVE_CODES` accepts `TY`.** The gateway comment says the wheel validates it and *"the gateway never bypasses that check"*; `016` T003 added TY to the Workbench's own `EXPORT_PERSPECTIVE_CODES`, which is a different list. **If the wheel refuses TY, the stats route is closed and the answer has to come from the loss table itself.**
2. **Whether the capture point is early enough.** `31` **O31-11** is still open and compounds here: `resolved.treaties` is written only at retrieval, so **every analysis retrieved before 9/16 already shows no TY**, and a has-loss flag stored the same way inherits the same backfill problem.
3. **What happens when the flag is unknown** — a read that failed, or an analysis captured before the flag existed. **Fail open (show the treaty) or fail closed (hide it)?** Cheryl's *"if I don't see it in the list, that's a flag for me"* argues for fail-open with a marker, since a silently hidden treaty is indistinguishable from a zero-loss one.

→ **O32-2**.
